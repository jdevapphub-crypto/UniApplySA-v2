How APS Works

Each of your six best subjects is awarded points based on your achievement level: 90–100% = 7 points, 80–89% = 6, 70–79% = 5, 60–69% = 4, 50–59% = 3, 40–49% = 2, and 30–39% = 1. Your APS is the sum of these points.

Most universities require a minimum APS of 24–30 for diploma programmes, and 30–42 for degree programmes. Competitive courses like medicine, engineering, and law often require 38+.

Note: Some universities exclude Life Orientation, or only count it as 1 point. Always check the specific APS rules of your target institution.

NSFAS Application Guide 2026

The National Student Financial Aid Scheme (NSFAS) provides funding for eligible South African students at public universities and TVET colleges. This guide walks you through the 2026 application process.

Who Qualifies for NSFAS?

NSFAS funding is available to South African citizens from households with a combined annual income of R350,000 or less. Students with disabilities qualify with a household income of up to R600,000. You must be accepted or registered at a public university or TVET college.

NSFAS covers tuition, accommodation, transport, and a personal care allowance. It is a bursary, not a loan — you do not need to pay it back.

Required Documents

  • Certified copy of your ID (or birth certificate if under 16)
  • Certified copies of parents' or guardians' IDs
  • Proof of household income (payslips, SASSA letter, or affidavit)
  • Consent form signed by parents/guardians
  • Latest academic results
  • Disability evidence (if applicable)

How to Apply

  1. Visit the official NSFAS portal at nsfas.org.za
  2. Create a myNSFAS account with your ID number and email
  3. Complete the online application form
  4. Upload certified supporting documents
  5. Submit and track your application status
⚠️ Important: NSFAS applications for 2026 typically open in September 2025 and close in January 2026. Apply early to avoid delays.

How to Apply Online

Applying to a South African university or TVET college is now done online. Follow this step-by-step guide to complete your 2026 application successfully.

Step 1: Choose Your Institution

Decide which universities or TVET colleges you want to apply to. You can apply to multiple institutions. Check the courses offered, APS requirements, and location.

Step 2: Gather Your Documents

Before starting, scan or photograph certified copies of your ID, NSC results, proof of residence, and proof of income (for NSFAS). Save them as PDF or JPEG files.

Step 3: Create an Account

Go to the institution's official website and create an applicant account. You'll need a valid email address and South African ID number.

Step 4: Complete the Application

Fill in your personal details, academic history, and course choices. Upload your documents. Pay the application fee (if required) via EFT or card.

Step 5: Submit and Track

Submit your application and save your reference number. Check your email and the applicant portal regularly for updates. Some institutions may request additional documents.

TVET Colleges Application 2026

TVET (Technical and Vocational Education and Training) colleges offer practical, career-focused qualifications in fields like engineering, business, hospitality, and IT. There are 52 public TVET colleges in South Africa.

Entry Requirements

For NC(V) programmes, you need Grade 9. For Report 191 (NATED) programmes, you need Grade 12. Some courses have specific subject requirements.

How to Apply

Applications are usually done online via each college's website, or in person at the campus. You'll need your ID, latest results, and proof of residence. NSFAS funding is available for qualifying students.

    UNISA Application 2026

    The University of South Africa (UNISA) is the largest open distance learning institution in Africa. Applications for 2026 typically open in September and close in October.

    How to Apply to UNISA

    1. Visit the official UNISA website at unisa.ac.za
    2. Click on "Apply for Admission"

    4. Complete the online application form

    • NSC results or equivalent

    Check Your Application Status

    After submitting your application, you can track its progress online through the institution's applicant portal. Here's how.

    How to Check Status

    1. Go to the institution's official website
    2. Click on "Check Application Status" or "Applicant Portal"
    3. Log in with your ID number and student/reference number
    4. View your application status (Pending, Accepted, Rejected, Waitlisted)
    5. Check your email regularly for updates

    What the Statuses Mean

    • Pending: Your application is being processed.
    • Accepted: Congratulations! You've been admitted.
    • Waitlisted: You may be admitted if space becomes available.
    • Rejected: Unfortunately, you did not meet the requirements.
    • Conditional: Admission depends on final results.
